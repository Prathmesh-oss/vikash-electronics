Project Introduction

Vikash Electronics is a specialized electronic repair workshop that currently depends on offline word-of-mouth and localized walk-in customers across Surat.

This project is an electronic repair showcase and management portal built exclusively for Vikash Electronics, listing its full spectrum of repair capabilities with prices, turnaround details, and authentic workshop bench imagery.

Customers can browse specialized services (Speakers, Power Amplifiers, Sharpy Moving Head Lights, Sound Mixers, and TVs) and contact the technician directly through one-click phone calls or WhatsApp without going through any third-party app.

The site also carries the workshop's verified physical address in Limbayat, Google Maps directions, and contact number in one place.

The goal is to provide a self-owned authoritative digital presence for Vikash Electronics and eliminate reliance on generic third-party business directories.

Objective(s)

Build a single, self-owned website where customers can view the full repair service spectrum with technical highlights and genuine photos
Allow customers to initiate direct phone inquiries and instant WhatsApp consultations with the technician directly through the website
Reduce Vikash Electronics's dependency on third-party aggregators and directories (Justdial, Indiamart) and eliminate commission charges
Display business information — location, address, contact number, and operating hours — in one place
Provide the workshop technician with a simple way to present high-end repair capabilities (Sharpy beam calibration, voice coils, transistor pairs)
Improve customer discovery and business inquiries across Surat and South Gujarat through structured Schema.org LocalBusiness SEO

Software Development Model

(SDLC Models Overview & Selection)

Importance of SDLC in Web Engineering:

Selecting the appropriate Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) model determines project predictability, risk mitigation, customer collaboration frequency, and software delivery quality for Vikash Electronics.

Key SDLC Models Evaluated for this Project:
  • 1. Waterfall Model: Classical Linear Sequential Model
  • 2. Prototype Model: Iterative Mockups & Client Feedback Loops
  • 3. V-Model: Verification and Validation Model with mapped testing phases
  • 4. Spiral Model: Risk-Driven Evolutionary SDLC
  • 5. Agile (Scrum) Model: Incremental Sprints & Bi-Weekly Deliveries [SELECTED]
Evaluation Parameters for Vikash Electronics:

Requirement volatility, workshop client availability for reviews, fast time-to-market before Gujarat festival season, and high mobile usability.

Software Development Model

(Spiral Model: Risk-Driven)

Quadrant 1: Objective Setting

Define business goals, alternatives, and operational constraints for the web portal.

Quadrant 2: Risk Analysis & Prototyping

Identify technical and commercial risks (e.g., SEO visibility, mobile UX adoption).

Quadrant 4: Review & Plan Next Phase

Evaluate client feedback, review milestones, and plan the next evolutionary cycle.

Quadrant 3: Engineering & Testing

Develop Next.js components, integrate APIs, and conduct verification tests.

Evaluation for Vikash Electronics: The Spiral Model excels in massive, high-budget, high-uncertainty systems. While we leveraged its risk-analysis principles to mitigate local SEO and mobile usability risks, the overall overhead is excessive for a small-business web platform.

Software Development Model

(Agile Scrum Model: Selected Approach)

Why Agile Scrum was Chosen for Vikash Electronics:

• Iterative Sprints: Allows delivering functional increments (e.g. Hero & Services first, then interactive Maps & SEO) without waiting for full completion.

• Continuous Stakeholder Collaboration: Bi-weekly review demos with Mr. Murlidhar Chaudhari to incorporate newly photographed workshop equipment.

Modeling

(Developing the use cases)

I. Who are the primary and secondary actors? Primary: DJs, Sound Rental Vendors, Event Planners. Secondary: Chief Technician.
II. What are the actor's goal(s)? Quickly find repair services for faulty gear, verify technician credentials, and initiate contact.
III. What preconditions should exist before the story begins? Customer has active internet connection and a browser.
IV. What main tasks or functions are performed by the actor? Browse services, inspect fault diagnostics, call technician, chat via WhatsApp, locate shop.
V. What exceptions might be considered? Device lacks phone dialer (system offers copy-to-clipboard or WhatsApp).
VI. What variations in interaction are possible? Direct call vs WhatsApp Web redirect; category-filtered view vs full list.
VII. What system information will the actor acquire? Repair specializations, diagnostic highlights, operating hours, GPS coordinates.
VIII. External environment changes? Owner updates phone numbers or adds new equipment specializations via data layer.
IX. Information desired by actor? Turnaround feasibility, technician competence, exact workshop address in Limbayat.
X. Informed of unexpected changes? Holiday workshop closures or emergency on-call notices displayed in status banner.
